\begin{abstract}
Edge servers that act on a monitored process need an estimate that is correct, not merely recent. In
satellite-ground integrated networks (SGINs), satellite handover and shadowing interrupt links for long enough that the
monitored process can change state several times, so we adopt the age of incorrect information (AoII) as the measure
of estimation quality. We formulate VANGUARD, a robust network-level problem that chooses update rates, serving-node
association, and bandwidth to minimize the worst-case time-average AoII over a set of disruption scenarios, and solve
it with SENTINEL, a distributed hybrid quantum-classical procedure. A renewal-interval decomposition turns the
history-dependent AoII into a closed-form cost per inter-delivery interval; the minimax is dualized into classically
updated scenario weights, so that each satellite-ground domain solves a linear assignment subproblem encoded as a
compact quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) problem whose one-hot structure is enforced by XY mixers;
the domains are coordinated by ADMM, and every schedule is certified by its exact worst-case AoII. The evaluation uses
Starlink ephemerides, the 3GPP TR 38.811 channel, acknowledgement feedback for all policies, an exact
integer-programming reference, and paired tests over 30 to 60 seeds. The certificate held in 98.7\% of orchestration
epochs under real passes, the robust schedule's certificate is 2 to 7\% below the nominal schedule's at no measurable
cost in realized AoII, and SENTINEL with a state-aware trigger comes within 10 to 20\% of the best per-slot heuristic
while retaining the certificate. The compact formulation needs four to six times fewer qubits than a direct epigraph
encoding, and thirteen domains of 8 to 38 qubits sampled on two IBM Heron processors decoded to the exact optimum; at
these sizes the quantum sampler is correct but not a better optimizer than greedy association, and we state the scale
at which that would change.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%=======================================================================

Sixth-generation networks are expected to place decision-making at the edge~\cite{Saad2020}: an edge server that
monitors a hazard indicator, the integrity of a structure, or the position of an asset must act on its own estimate of
that process, often without a human in the loop. What matters to such a server is whether its estimate is still
correct. Terrestrial mobile edge computing provides capacity and low delay where ground infrastructure exists, which is
often not where monitoring is most needed, and satellite links extend coverage to the remaining regions at the price of
intermittent connectivity~\cite{Giordani2021}. The combination, a satellite-ground integrated network (SGIN), is the
setting of this paper. We write SGIN rather than the more common SAGIN because the system has no aerial layer: there
are no UAVs or high-altitude platforms, only satellites and ground nodes.

Satellite links fail in ways that terrestrial links rarely do. A low-earth-orbit satellite sets and hands the user over
to the next one every few minutes; buildings and foliage block the line of sight for the length of a pass; shadow
fading in the non-line-of-sight state exceeds ten decibels~\cite{TR38811}. During any of these interruptions the
monitored process keeps evolving, and by the time the link is restored the edge estimate may be wrong by several
states rather than simply old. The age of information (AoI)~\cite{Kaul2012, Yates2021} measures only elapsed time and
cannot tell these two situations apart. The age of incorrect information (AoII)~\cite{Maatouk2020, Maatouk2023} can:
it is zero while the estimate is correct and grows with the duration and the magnitude of a mismatch. AoII has been
analyzed mainly for one source and one monitor on a point-to-point channel. Carrying it to a network raises two
difficulties: the metric depends on the whole trajectory of the source since the estimate was last correct, so it does
not fit the per-slot cost structure of the assignment problems used for SGIN resource allocation; and the duration
of a mismatch is governed by outages of uncertain length, so an allocation must be judged by its worst case over
plausible disruptions rather than by its average.

We treat the problem as robust combinatorial optimization. A renewal-interval decomposition shows that a Markov source
with known transition matrix accumulates, between two deliveries, an expected AoII that depends only on the length of
the interval and can be tabulated offline, so the channel enters the objective through a single number per scenario
and the resulting robust scheduling problem, VANGUARD, is a minimax assignment problem over binary variables. The
question is then how such a problem can be given to a quantum sampler. A direct encoding of the minimax, with an
epigraph register and a slack register per scenario on top of every admissible decision variable, needs four to six
times the qubits of the encoding proposed here and produces a penalty landscape on which classical and quantum local
search alike fail to find a feasible point; we document this in Section~VI. SENTINEL, for Satellite-ground Edge
Network orchestration for Trustworthy Information via Node-level Estimation Limits, instead dualizes the minimax into
scenario weights that a classical outer loop updates, so that each inner subproblem is linear and is encoded as a
compact QUBO over the decision variables and a few capacity-slack bits; the one-hot constraint of each user is
enforced by the XY mixer of the circuit rather than by a penalty~\cite{Hadfield2019}. Satellite-ground domains are
coordinated by the sharing form of ADMM~\cite{Boyd2011}, and every assembled schedule is evaluated exactly under
every scenario, so the reported worst case is a certificate that does not rest on the solver's optimality. The
procedure is hybrid at every level: a classical optimizer tunes the circuit angles; the scenario weights, the
coordination, the repair, and the certificate are classical; and domains beyond the sampler's capacity are solved by
a classical annealer on the same QUBO. Section~VI states which solver produced each result.

The evaluation is built so that a reader can trust it: Starlink ephemerides propagated with SGP4~\cite{Vallado2006},
the suburban channel of 3GPP TR 38.811~\cite{TR38811}, acknowledgement feedback for every policy, an exact
integer-programming reference, common random episodes with paired tests, and runs of the quantum step on two IBM
Heron processors. Its findings are modest and precisely stated. The worst-case certificate held in 98.7\% of
orchestration epochs under real passes; the robust schedule's certificate is 2 to 7\% below its nominal counterpart's
in 67 to 100\% of seeds, while the two are statistically indistinguishable in realized AoII, so the value of the
scenario set is the guarantee, not a lower average. A per-slot policy that transmits whenever its observed AoII is
positive is the strongest heuristic when feedback is available and coincides with the Whittle-index
scheduler~\cite{Whittle1988, Kadota2018} at these bandwidths; SENTINEL with the same trigger comes within 10 to 20\%
of it while keeping the certificate, and is 25 to 30\% better than it at 15 to 20 users without feedback. The compact
formulation matches the exact optimum on the 10- and 25-UE instances with four to six times fewer qubits than the
epigraph encoding, and thirteen domains of 8 to 38 qubits sampled on \texttt{ibm\_kingston} and \texttt{ibm\_fez}
decoded to the exact optimum. On every instance that fits a current device greedy association is already optimal, so
the quantum step is a correct, hardware-runnable constrained sampler and not yet a better optimizer; we quantify this
per shot against a uniform-sampling baseline and state the scale at which it would change.

The contributions of this paper are the following.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=13pt]
\item The robust scheduling problem VANGUARD, which minimizes the worst-case time-average AoII of a network of
monitored edge nodes over a set of satellite shadowing and handover scenarios, jointly over update rates,
association, and bandwidth, under capacity, visibility, rate, and AoII-budget constraints.
\item A renewal-interval decomposition that expresses the expected AoII accumulated between deliveries in closed form
and reconciles the history dependence of AoII with binary-variable optimization; verified against Monte Carlo
simulation to within one percent.
\item SENTINEL, a distributed hybrid quantum-classical procedure that dualizes the minimax into classically updated scenario weights,
encodes each domain's subproblem as a compact QUBO with XY-mixer-enforced one-hot structure, prunes dominated
candidates, and coordinates the domains by ADMM with a warm start and a cost-scaled penalty, with a certificate of
the worst-case AoII of every schedule.
\item An evaluation under Starlink ephemerides and the TR 38.811 channel against an exact reference, with paired
statistics, Whittle-index and trained learning baselines, an ablation of failure feedback, sensitivity studies, and
runs of the quantum step on two superconducting processors with its scale limit stated explicitly.
\end{itemize}

Section~II reviews related work. Section~III presents the system model, Section~IV formulates VANGUARD and its
binary reformulations, Section~V develops the dualized compact QUBO, the XY-mixer QAOA, and the ADMM coordination,
Section~VI reports the evaluation, and Section~VII concludes.

\section{State of the Art}
%=======================================================================

\subsubsection{Satellite-Ground Integrated Edge Computing}
Mobile edge computing~\cite{Mao2017} has been extended beyond terrestrial coverage by placing edge servers on
satellites and base stations~\cite{Liu2018, Kato2019, Zhang2019}, with task offloading under deterministic or
average channel assumptions as the dominant problem~\cite{Cheng2019, Zhou2021} and completion delay as the objective;
the correctness of what the server believes about the monitored process has not been the quantity optimized. The
3GPP study on non-terrestrial networks~\cite{TR38811} provides elevation-dependent line-of-sight probabilities,
clutter losses, and shadow-fading statistics, and SGP4 with public two-line elements~\cite{Vallado2006} gives pass
geometry for any constellation; most SGIN evaluations use neither, although with real passes the dominant
disruptions are handovers and non-line-of-sight passes rather than coverage gaps.

\subsubsection{Age of Information and Age of Incorrect Information}
AoI is a widely studied but content-agnostic timeliness measure~\cite{Kaul2012, Kosta2017, Sun2017, Kadota2018,
Yates2021}. AoII~\cite{Maatouk2020} penalizes the duration and magnitude of estimation error and has been analyzed
for a single source under threshold and semi-Markov policies~\cite{Maatouk2023, Kriouile2021} and over unreliable,
power-constrained channels~\cite{Chen2021}. For several sources over shared channels the standard tool is the Whittle
index of the restless bandit~\cite{Whittle1988}, applied to AoI by Kadota et al.~\cite{Kadota2018}, and we include
it as a baseline. Missing is a formulation in
which association, bandwidth, and update rates are chosen jointly across heterogeneous serving nodes, under outages
that span several source transitions, with a guarantee on the worst case.

\subsubsection{Robust Allocation and Learning-Based Scheduling}
Robust optimization minimizes cost against the worst realization of an uncertain parameter in a bounded
set~\cite{BenTal2009, Bertsimas2004}. When the uncertainty set is a finite list of scenarios, the minimax is a
maximization over scenario weights in the simplex, and the saddle point can be approached by multiplicative-weights
updates~\cite{Arora2012}; this is the mechanism we use to keep the quantum subproblem linear. Robust allocation in
satellite-ground edge computing has treated uncertainty as a perturbation of rate or delay, not as the driver of
estimation error. Deep reinforcement learning~\cite{Mnih2015, Schulman2017} has been applied to AoI- and AoII-aware
scheduling in small systems; it optimizes an expected return and gives no bound on the estimation error under
disruptions outside its training distribution. That does not disqualify it as a baseline, and we train one with the
same reward, feedback, and episodes as every other policy.

\subsubsection{Quantum-Assisted Combinatorial Optimization}
QUBO formulations~\cite{Lucas2014} solved by QAOA~\cite{Farhi2014} or by annealing~\cite{Albash2018} have been proposed for channel assignment and scheduling in wireless
networks~\cite{Kim2019}. Two developments bear on constrained assignment: the alternating-operator form of
QAOA~\cite{Hadfield2019}, whose mixer preserves a constraint subspace so that one-hot constraints need no penalty
terms, and the concentration of optimal QAOA parameters across instances~\cite{Brandao2018}, which allows angles
optimized on a small instance to be reused on a larger one; error suppression by dynamical
decoupling~\cite{Viola1999} and randomized compiling~\cite{Wallman2016} is inexpensive, and we report its effect.
QAOA has not been applied to a correctness objective, and reports of its use for network resource allocation rarely
state at what size the quantum sampler stops being competitive with a classical heuristic.

\subsubsection{Research Gap}
No existing work formulates AoII as a robust minimax multi-node scheduling problem with joint association and
resource allocation, targets information correctness rather than delay in robust SGIN allocation, or applies
quantum-assisted optimization to a correctness objective with an encoding a current device can execute. SENTINEL
addresses these three points.

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:model}
%=======================================================================

\begin{figure}[!t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/system_model.pdf}
\caption{System model: two satellite-ground domains with their base stations, shared LEO satellites, monitored
sources $X_i(t)$, and the classical coordinator. Solid: BS link with acknowledgement (dotted); dashed: satellite
link; dash-dotted: satellite link blocked during a non-line-of-sight pass.}
\label{Fig1}
\end{figure}

We consider a satellite-ground integrated edge network in which monitoring edge nodes (UEs) observe time-varying
physical processes and transmit status updates to edge servers hosted at terrestrial base stations (BSs) or at
visible low-earth-orbit satellites (SATs). Let $\mathscr{U}$, $\mathscr{S}$, and $\mathscr{B}$ denote the sets of UEs,
SATs, and BSs, indexed by $u_i$, $s_j$, and $b_n$, and let $\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{S}\cup\mathscr{B}$ be the set of
serving nodes. Each serving node hosts an edge server that keeps a running estimate of every process assigned to it
and acts on that estimate. Time is slotted with slot duration $T_s$. UEs have low mobility over a decision interval;
the satellite topology changes continuously because of orbital motion. Table~\ref{tab:notation} lists the notation.

In contrast to the task-offloading models that dominate SGIN edge computing, the quantity of interest here is the
correctness of the server's estimate. It depends on the dynamics of the monitored process, on when the UE samples and
transmits, and on whether the transmission is delivered before the process moves again. Satellite handover and
shadowing make the last of these uncertain, and an allocation chosen under nominal channel conditions can leave the
server with an estimate that is wrong rather than late.

The network is partitioned into $K$ local satellite-ground domains $\mathcal{D}_k=(\mathscr{U}_k,\mathscr{S}_k,
\mathscr{B}_k)$, $k\in\mathscr{K}=\{1,\ldots,K\}$, where the UE subsets partition $\mathscr{U}$ and a SAT may be
visible to several domains. Each domain has a classical controller that builds and solves a local robust binary
problem with a hybrid QAOA solver; a classical coordinator reconciles the bandwidth of SATs shared across domains and
certifies every assembled network schedule by its exact worst-case AoII.

\subsection{Satellite Geometry and Communications Model}
\label{sec:geometry}

Satellite positions are obtained from a constellation ephemeris; the evaluation propagates a public two-line element
set with SGP4~\cite{Vallado2006}, but the model only requires, for every SAT $s_j$ and UE $u_i$, the slant range
$d^{us}_{ij}(t)$ and the elevation $V_{ij}(t)$ in each slot. A SAT can serve $u_i$ in slot $t$ only if
$V_{ij}(t)\geq\Theta$. A UE keeps its serving SAT until it sets below $\Theta$ and is then handed over to the SAT
with the highest elevation, so a dense constellation yields passes of several minutes separated by handover
interruptions and no coverage gaps at the masks considered here.

The nominal path loss (in dB) between $u_i$ and $s_j$ is
\begin{equation}
    \varphi^{us}_{ij}=\varsigma^{us}+\zeta^{us}\log_{10}\!\left(f_{c}^{us}\right)
    +\xi^{us}\log_{10}\!\left(d^{us}_{ij}\right)+\lambda^{\mathrm{atm}}(V_{ij}),
\end{equation}
with carrier frequency $f_c^{us}$, the reference loss and exponents $\varsigma^{us}$, $\zeta^{us}$, $\xi^{us}$ of
free-space propagation, and the atmospheric absorption $\lambda^{\mathrm{atm}}(V)\propto 1/\sin V$. The UE-BS path
loss $\varphi^{ub}_{in}$ uses the ground distance $d^{ub}_{in}$, a terrestrial exponent $\xi^{ub}>2$, and a fixed
margin. With transmit power $p_i$ and antenna gains $G^{u}$ and $G^{s}$ at the terminal and the satellite, the nominal
uplink rate with bandwidth $\beta^{us}_{ij}$ is
\begin{equation}
    R^{us}_{ij}=\beta_{ij}^{us}\,\min\!\left\{\log_{2}\!\left(1+
    \frac{10^{(p_{i}+G^{u}+G^{s}-\varphi^{us}_{ij})/10}}{N_0\,\beta^{us}_{ij}\,F^{s}}\right),\ \bar{\gamma}\right\},
    \label{eq:rate_us}
\end{equation}
where $N_0$ is the noise power spectral density, $F^{s}$ the receiver noise figure, and $\bar\gamma$ the spectral
efficiency of the highest modulation and coding scheme; $R^{ub}_{in}$ is defined in the same way with $G^{b}$. Each
node divides its bandwidth among the UEs it serves, so a link's rate does not depend on other UEs' association once
the bandwidth level is fixed. Co-channel interference is absorbed in the noise figure, and residual Doppler, which
the NTN frequency pre-compensation removes to within the subcarrier spacing, is not modelled.

\emph{Large-scale channel.} Each satellite link also carries a large-scale state drawn once per pass from the
TR 38.811 model~\cite{TR38811}: with the elevation-dependent probability $p_{\mathrm{LOS}}(V)$ the pass is in line of
sight, otherwise it carries the clutter loss $\lambda^{\mathrm{cl}}(V)$ of the band (17 to 25~dB at Ku band and
suburban elevations); shadow fading with the state-dependent deviation $\sigma_{\mathrm{sf}}(V)$ is drawn per pass
and small-scale fading per slot. About one pass segment in twenty is in non-line-of-sight in the suburban environment
of Section~VI, and such a pass blocks the link for its whole duration at any bandwidth level.

\subsection{Monitored Source and Estimation Model}
\label{sec:source}

Each UE $u_i$ observes a process $X_i(t)$ modeled as a discrete-time Markov chain on a finite state space
$\mathcal{X}_i$ with transition matrix $P_i$, in which the states are ordered severity or status levels. The UE
controls a generation indicator $g_i(t)\in\{0,1\}$ that specifies whether an update is sampled and transmitted in
slot $t$. We assume that $T_s$ exceeds the transmission and propagation delay of a successful update, so that an update
sampled at the start of slot $t$ is delivered within that slot or lost. Let $t_{i,\kappa}$ be the $\kappa$-th slot in
which an update from $u_i$ is delivered and $Y_{i,\kappa}=t_{i,\kappa+1}-t_{i,\kappa}$ the inter-delivery interval.
The server assigned to $u_i$ holds the estimate
\begin{equation}
    \hat{X}_i(t)=X_i(t_{i,\kappa}),\qquad t_{i,\kappa}\leq t<t_{i,\kappa+1},
\end{equation}
which is fixed between deliveries while the process keeps moving. With $\Lambda_i(t)=\max\{\tau\leq t:
X_i(\tau)=\hat{X}_i(\tau)\}$ the most recent slot in which the estimate was correct, the instantaneous AoII
is~\cite{Maatouk2020}
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_i^{II}(t)=\bigl(t-\Lambda_i(t)\bigr)\,f_i\!\left(X_i(t),\hat{X}_i(t)\right),
    \label{eq:aoii_def}
\end{equation}
where $f_i(x,\hat{x})\geq 0$ with $f_i(x,x)=0$ measures the severity of a mismatch; we use $f_i(x,\hat{x})=|x-\hat{x}|$.
AoII returns to zero without a delivery whenever the process revisits the estimated state.

\subsection{Disruption Scenarios}
\label{sec:scenarios}

The orchestrator plans against a finite scenario set $\mathscr{L}=\{1,\ldots,L\}$. Scenario $\ell$ adds a loss
$\delta^{us}_{ij}(\ell)$ to every UE-SAT link for a window of $T^{\mathrm{dis}}(\ell)$ slots,
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\varphi}^{us}_{ij}(\ell)=\varphi^{us}_{ij}+\delta^{us}_{ij}(\ell),
    \label{eq:realized_pathloss}
\end{equation}
the scenario rate $R^{us}_{ij}(\ell)$ follows from~\eqref{eq:rate_us}, and an update sampled in slot $t$ is delivered
under scenario $\ell$ only if $R^{us}_{ij}(\ell)\,T_s\geq D_i$ and $V_{ij}(t)\geq\Theta$, where $D_i$ is the update
size in bits. BS links are unaffected. We use five scenarios: the nominal channel; a deep shadowing event in which
every satellite link loses $\sigma_{\mathrm{sf}}$ of the non-line-of-sight state, 12~dB in the suburban tables, for a
window of $T^{\mathrm{dis}}$ slots; the handover interruption of each individual satellite, a complete outage of that
link for the duration of a handover; and the joint outage of all satellites for the deep-shadowing window. A mild 6~dB
scenario was dropped after the sensitivity study of Section~VI showed that it never binds; the handover scenarios are
kept per satellite because the hedge appears only once the second shared satellite's handover is in the set.

A scenario thus determines the length of the outage window and therefore the inter-delivery interval
$Y_{i,\kappa}(\ell)$, the number of slots from $t_{i,\kappa}$ until the next slot in which $g_i(t)=1$ and the delivery
condition holds; a longer window lets $X_i(t)$ pass through more states. The scenario set is the planner's model of
the disruption process, whereas the simulator of Section~VI draws its disruptions from the TR 38.811 state process
and the ephemeris; the agreement between the two is what the certificate's validity measures.

\subsection{Renewal-Interval AoII Decomposition}
\label{sec:renewal}

Because $\Delta_i^{II}(t)$ depends on the trajectory of $X_i(t)$ since the estimate was last correct, it is not a
per-slot function of the channel state and cannot be used directly as the cost coefficient of a binary variable. The
delivery process is, however, a renewal process. Under the same-slot delivery assumption the estimate is exact at each
delivery, so $\Delta_i^{II}(t_{i,\kappa})=0$, and between two deliveries the estimate is fixed at
$\hat{x}=X_i(t_{i,\kappa})$ while the AoII evolves under the source's own dynamics, independently of the channel.

Let $\tilde{P}_{i,\hat{x}}=P_i\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{e}_{\hat{x}}\mathbf{e}_{\hat{x}}^{\top}\bigr)$ be the taboo
matrix with the $\hat{x}$-th column of $P_i$ set to zero, so that $[\tilde{P}_{i,\hat{x}}^{a}]_{\hat{x}y}$ is the
probability of moving from $\hat{x}$ to $y$ in $\iota$ steps without returning to $\hat{x}$, and let
$\mathbf{f}_{i,\hat{x}}$ be the vector with entries $f_i(y,\hat{x})$. The AoII $s$ slots after a delivery equals
$\iota\,f_i(y,\hat{x})$ exactly when the source was last at $\hat{x}$ at slot $s-\iota$ and has since moved to $y$
without returning, so
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta_i^{II}(t_{i,\kappa}+s)\,\middle|\,\hat{x}\right]
    =\sum_{\iota=1}^{s} \iota\,\bigl[P_i^{\,s-\iota}\bigr]_{\hat{x}\hat{x}}
    \bigl[\tilde{P}_{i,\hat{x}}^{\,\iota}\,\mathbf{f}_{i,\hat{x}}\bigr]_{\hat{x}},
    \label{eq:aoii_slot}
\end{equation}
and the expected AoII accumulated over an interval of length $Y$ is
\begin{align}
    \Psi_i(Y\mid\hat{x})&=\sum_{s=1}^{Y-1}
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta_i^{II}(t_{i,\kappa}+s)\,\middle|\,\hat{x}\right],
    \nonumber\\
    \Psi_i(Y)&=\sum_{\hat{x}\in\mathcal{X}_i}\pi_i(\hat{x})\,\Psi_i(Y\mid\hat{x}),
    \label{eq:psi_def}
\end{align}
where $\pi_i$ is the stationary distribution of $P_i$. Averaging over $\pi_i$ is legitimate because the update
decisions do not depend on the realized source state, so the delivered state is stationary. By the renewal-reward
theorem the time-average AoII of $u_i$ when its deliveries are $Y$ slots apart is $\Psi_i(Y)/Y$. Both quantities depend
only on $Y$ and $P_i$ and are tabulated once per UE for $Y\leq Y^{\max}$; the table agrees with Monte Carlo
simulation of the source and delivery process over $1.5\times 10^{6}$ slots within one percent at every interval length
used.

Equation~\eqref{eq:psi_def} separates the source from the channel. The only channel-dependent quantity the planner
must reason about is the inter-delivery interval, which under scenario $\ell$ is deterministic for a given allocation
and is denoted $Y_i(\ell)$; the cost is the tabulated ratio $\Psi_i(Y_i(\ell))/Y_i(\ell)$.

\subsection{Binary Decision Model}
\label{sec:binary}

The update rates and bandwidths are continuous. We discretize the bandwidth of each node $m\in\mathscr{M}$ into $Q_m$
levels $\{\bar\beta_m^1,\ldots,\bar\beta_m^{Q_m}\}$ and the update rate of $u_i$ into $H_i$ levels
$\{\bar\rho_i^1,\ldots,\bar\rho_i^{H_i}\}\subset[\rho_i^{\min},\rho_i^{\max}]$, and define
\begin{equation}
    x_{imqh}=
    \begin{cases}
        1, & \text{if } u_i \text{ uses node } m\text{, level } q\text{, rate } h, \\
        0, & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:x_def}
\end{equation}
The original decisions are recovered as $\alpha_{ij}=\sum_{q,h}x_{ijqh}$, $\omega_{in}=\sum_{q,h}x_{inqh}$,
$\beta^{us}_{ij}=\sum_{q,h}\bar\beta_j^q\,x_{ijqh}$, $\beta^{ub}_{in}=\sum_{q,h}\bar\beta_n^q\,x_{inqh}$, and
$\rho_i=\sum_{m,q,h}\bar\rho_i^h\,x_{imqh}$. The scenario does not index the variable, because it is realized by the
environment rather than chosen; each variable instead carries $L$ precomputed cost coefficients. After the
inadmissible combinations are removed (Section~\ref{sec:reform}) the number of decision variables is
\begin{equation}
    N_{x}=\sum_{i\in\mathscr{U}}|\mathscr{A}_i|\;\leq\;\sum_{i\in\mathscr{U}}\sum_{m\in\mathscr{M}}Q_{m}H_{i},
    \label{eq:Nx}
\end{equation}
which grows linearly with the number of UEs, nodes, and levels. Section~V prunes each $\mathscr{A}_i$ further to a
candidate set $\mathscr{C}_i$ of the $\varkappa$ combinations with the smallest worst-case cost, which in our instances
never changed the optimum and reduced the qubit count by a further factor of two to three.

\subsection{Delivery Feedback and Link-Failure Handling}
\label{sec:feedback}

A serving node acknowledges each delivered update. A UE whose transmissions to a node have failed in every slot of a
window of $T^{\mathrm{f}}$ slots declares the link failed and avoids that node for $T^{\mathrm{b}}$ slots or, for a
satellite, until its next handover; its controller re-solves the domain problem without that link at the next
orchestration interval. The rule applies to every policy in Section~VI, since any state-aware baseline needs the same
information to be competitive; both regimes are reported.

\subsection{Distributed Orchestration Model}
\label{sec:orchestration}

Each domain $\mathcal{D}_k$ holds the local decision vector $\mathbf{x}_k=\{x_{imqh}\mid u_i\in\mathscr{U}_k,\,
(m,q,h)\in\mathscr{C}_i\}$ and the local state $\mathbf{s}_k=[\boldsymbol{\Psi}_k,\mathbf{V}_k,\mathbf{X}_k,
\mathbf{B}_k,\mathbf{E}_k,\boldsymbol{\Xi}_k]$, with $\boldsymbol{\Psi}_k$ the tabulated AoII costs of its UEs,
$\mathbf{V}_k$ the elevations, $\mathbf{X}_k$ the transition matrices and budgets, $\mathbf{B}_k$ the bandwidth levels,
$\mathbf{E}_k$ the ephemeris over the decision interval, and $\boldsymbol{\Xi}_k$ the scenario set restricted to the
domain. The controller re-solves whenever a satellite becomes visible or sets, when a link failure is reported, and
at least once per re-solve period. Within a solve, a classical outer loop maintains scenario weights, each domain
solves a linear assignment subproblem on a quantum sampler (or, when the domain exceeds the sampler's capacity, on a
classical annealer with the same QUBO), and the coordinator runs the sharing form of ADMM on the shared satellites'
bandwidth and evaluates the worst-case AoII of each assembled schedule exactly. The orchestration
latency extends every inter-delivery interval by $\epsilon_i$ slots and enters the cost through
$\Psi_i(Y_i(\ell)+\epsilon_i)$; in the evaluation it is measured, not assumed.

%=======================================================================
\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}
%=======================================================================

The objective is to minimize the worst-case time-average AoII across all monitored UEs over the scenario set, by
jointly choosing update rates, association, and bandwidth. Let $\boldsymbol{\chi}_i=\bigl(\rho_i,\{\alpha_{ij}\}_{j},
\{\omega_{in}\}_{n},\{\beta^{us}_{ij}\}_{j},\{\beta^{ub}_{in}\}_{n}\bigr)$ collect the decisions of $u_i$ and
$Y_i(\boldsymbol{\chi}_i,\boldsymbol{\xi})$ its inter-delivery interval under realization $\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\Omega$.
The robust Volatility-Aware Network-wide Generation, Update, Association, and Resource orchestration under
Disruption (VANGUARD) problem is
\begin{align}
    \mathscr{P}_{1}:\;&\min_{\{\boldsymbol{\chi}_i\}_{i\in\mathscr{U}}}
    \;\max_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\Omega}
    \;\frac{1}{|\mathscr{U}|}\sum_{i\in\mathscr{U}}
    \frac{\Psi_{i}\bigl(Y_{i}(\boldsymbol{\chi}_i,\boldsymbol{\xi})\bigr)}
    {Y_{i}(\boldsymbol{\chi}_i,\boldsymbol{\xi})}
    \nonumber\\
    \text{s.t.}\;\;
    &\text{C1}:\sum_{j\in\mathscr{S}}\alpha_{ij}+\sum_{n\in\mathscr{B}}\omega_{in}=1,\;\forall\,i\in\mathscr{U},
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{C2}:\sum_{i\in\mathscr{U}}\alpha_{ij}\,\beta^{us}_{ij}\leq B^{s}_{j},\;\forall\,j\in\mathscr{S},
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{C3}:\sum_{i\in\mathscr{U}}\omega_{in}\,\beta^{ub}_{in}\leq B^{b}_{n},\;\forall\,n\in\mathscr{B},
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{C4}:\frac{\Psi_{i}\bigl(Y_{i}(\boldsymbol{\chi}_i,\boldsymbol{\xi})\bigr)}
    {Y_{i}(\boldsymbol{\chi}_i,\boldsymbol{\xi})}\leq\Delta_{i}^{II,\max},
    \;\forall\,i\in\mathscr{U},\;\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\Omega,
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{C5}:\alpha_{ij}\bigl(V_{ij}-\Theta\bigr)\geq 0,\;\forall\,i\in\mathscr{U},\;\forall\,j\in\mathscr{S},
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{C6}:\rho_{i}^{\min}\leq\rho_{i}\leq\rho_{i}^{\max},\;\forall\,i\in\mathscr{U},
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{C7}:\alpha_{ij},\,\omega_{in}\in\{0,1\},\;\forall\,i,j,n.
    \nonumber
\end{align}
C1 routes each UE through exactly one serving node per decision interval. C2 and C3 are the bandwidth capacities. C4
is the correctness constraint: the expected time-average AoII of every UE stays within its budget
$\Delta_i^{II,\max}$ under every realization, including the worst. It is stated in expectation because the
instantaneous AoII of a Markov source cannot be bounded deterministically; the source may stay in a mismatched state
for a whole interval. C5 restricts association to visible satellites, C6 bounds the update rate by the freshness
requirement and the UE's energy or channel-access budget, and C7 makes association binary.

$\mathscr{P}_{1}$ is a mixed-integer nonlinear minimax program. With a single scenario and fixed rates it contains the
generalized assignment problem and is NP-hard. The inner maximization adds a difficulty of its own: because $\Psi_i$
is a nonlinear, source-specific function of the interval, the realization that maximizes the network AoII is not
necessarily the one with the worst channel but depends on how each source's volatility interacts with the outage it
induces; and the association set defined by C5 changes at every decision interval as satellites move.

\subsection{Robust Binary Reformulation}
\label{sec:reform}

With the discretization of Section~\ref{sec:binary} and the variable $x_{imqh}$ of~\eqref{eq:x_def}, each scenario $\ell$ gives,
for every UE-node pair and bandwidth level, a rate $R_{imq}(\ell)$ from~\eqref{eq:rate_us} with the scenario path
loss. The link is in outage throughout the scenario window if that rate cannot deliver an update within a slot, so the
induced outage is
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{imq}(\ell)=
    \begin{cases}
        T^{\mathrm{dis}}(\ell)\,\mathbb{I}\bigl\{R_{imq}(\ell)\,T_s<D_i\bigr\}, & m\in\mathscr{S},\\
        0, & m\in\mathscr{B}.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:tau_out}
\end{equation}
The bandwidth level matters: a larger allocation can keep the rate above the delivery threshold through deep
shadowing, whereas a handover interruption is an outage at any bandwidth. A UE whose update fails during an outage
transmits a fresh sample as soon as the link returns. The worst case is an outage that begins at a scheduled sampling
slot, which gives
\begin{equation}
    Y_{imqh}(\ell)=\left\lceil\frac{1}{\bar\rho_i^{h}T_s}\right\rceil+\tau_{imq}(\ell),\qquad
    \psi_{imqh}^{(\ell)}=\frac{\Psi_{i}\bigl(Y_{imqh}(\ell)\bigr)}{Y_{imqh}(\ell)},
    \label{eq:psi_imqh}
\end{equation}
precomputed for every $(i,m,q,h,\ell)$; with orchestration overhead, $Y_{imqh}(\ell)+\epsilon_i$ replaces
$Y_{imqh}(\ell)$.

\emph{Pre-elimination.} The admissible set $\mathscr{A}_i$ consists of the combinations $(m,q,h)$ for which (i)
$m\in\mathscr{B}$, or $m\in\mathscr{S}$ with $V_{im}\geq\Theta$; (ii) the nominal rate satisfies
$R_{imq}T_s\geq D_i$; and (iii) $\max_{\ell}\psi^{(\ell)}_{imqh}\leq\Delta_i^{II,\max}$. Condition (i) enforces C5
and (iii) is the discrete form of C4: since each UE selects exactly one combination, it meets its budget under every
scenario if and only if its selection lies in $\mathscr{A}_i$, so the budget is enforced by removing variables rather
than by penalty terms, at no cost in qubits. A UE with empty $\mathscr{A}_i$ (a satellite-only UE whose budget lies
below the AoII of a full handover outage) is assigned its least-bad combination and reported as a budget violation.

The binary reformulation of VANGUARD is
\begin{align}
    \mathscr{P}_{2}:\;&\min_{\mathbf{x}}\;\max_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\;
    \frac{1}{|\mathscr{U}|}\sum_{i\in\mathscr{U}}\sum_{(m,q,h)\in\mathscr{A}_i}\psi_{imqh}^{(\ell)}\,x_{imqh}
    \nonumber\\
    \text{s.t.}\;\;
    &\text{D1}:\sum_{(m,q,h)\in\mathscr{A}_i}x_{imqh}=1,\;\forall\,i\in\mathscr{U},
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{D2}:\sum_{i\in\mathscr{U}}\sum_{(j,q,h)\in\mathscr{A}_i}\bar{\beta}^{q}_{j}\,x_{ijqh}\leq B^{s}_{j},
    \;\forall\,j\in\mathscr{S},
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{D3}:\sum_{i\in\mathscr{U}}\sum_{(n,q,h)\in\mathscr{A}_i}\bar{\beta}^{q}_{n}\,x_{inqh}\leq B^{b}_{n},
    \;\forall\,n\in\mathscr{B},
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{D4}:x_{imqh}\in\{0,1\},\;\forall\,i\in\mathscr{U},\;(m,q,h)\in\mathscr{A}_i.
    \nonumber
\end{align}
D1 is the one-hot selection, D2 and D3 the discrete capacities, D4 the binary domain.

\subsection{Epigraph Form, Certificate, and Exact Reference}
\label{sec:epigraph}

Introducing the worst-case value $\eta$ gives the equivalent epigraph form
\begin{align}
    \mathscr{P}_{3}:\;&\min_{\mathbf{x},\,\eta}\;\eta
    \nonumber\\
    \text{s.t.}\;\;&\text{E1}:\frac{1}{|\mathscr{U}|}\sum_{i\in\mathscr{U}}\sum_{(m,q,h)\in\mathscr{A}_i}
    \psi_{imqh}^{(\ell)}\,x_{imqh}\leq\eta,\;\forall\,\ell\in\mathscr{L},
    \nonumber\\
    &\text{D1--D4},
    \nonumber
\end{align}
in which E1 is tight for at least one scenario at the optimum. $\mathscr{P}_{3}$ serves two purposes in this paper,
neither of which is to be solved on a quantum device. First, it is the certificate: for any schedule $\mathbf{x}$
returned by any solver, the $L$ left-hand sides of E1 are evaluated exactly, so the worst-case time-average AoII of
that schedule over $\mathscr{L}$ is known with certainty. Second, $\mathscr{P}_{3}$ is a mixed-integer linear program,
and up to 75 UEs it is solved to proven optimality by a branch-and-cut solver~\cite{HiGHS2018} in well under a
second, with a time limit reached only on some 100-UE instances; this gives an exact reference for every heuristic and
quantum solver we compare. A direct
QUBO encoding of $\mathscr{P}_{3}$, with $\eta$ on a binary grid and a slack register for each of the $L$
constraints E1, was the encoding of an earlier version of this work; it multiplies the qubit count by four to six
and produces a penalty landscape in which neither simulated annealing nor QAOA found a feasible assignment on
instances of ten UEs. Section~V therefore works with $\mathscr{P}_{2}$ directly, replacing the inner maximization by
scenario weights so that every quantum subproblem is linear in $\mathbf{x}$ and the only auxiliary qubits are the
slack bits of the capacity constraints D2 and D3.
